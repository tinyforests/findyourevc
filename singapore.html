01 · Original system

Lowland dipterocarp forest

The dominant primeval forest system across much of Singapore: tall, layered tropical rainforest with emergent trees, dense canopy, understorey and complex vertical habitat.

03 · Ecological opportunity

Rebuild the layers

Where a former forest site is now urban or highly managed, the opportunity is not to recreate a rainforest literally. It is to return some of its structure and function: canopy, sub-canopy, shrubs, ground layer, organic matter, shade, water retention and habitat.

Design translation · not a classification
The shift in question
Not only “what should we plant?”
but “what ecological system did this place belong to — and what can it contribute again?”

Ecological reference species.

A small set of Singapore-native species associated with the selected vegetation community. These are ecological references, not a site-specific planting specification — scale, hydrology, soil, exposure and conservation status still matter.

01 / Original

Primeval vegetation

The historic layer interprets Singapore through reconstructed lowland dipterocarp forest, freshwater swamp forest and mangrove forest. It is a way of recovering ecological context before modern urbanisation.

Yee et al. 2011 ↗
02 / Present

Land cover · 2018

Gaw, Yee & Richards mapped ecologically relevant vegetation, freshwater and built-surface classes using high-resolution satellite imagery from 2003–2018. The published map reported 79% overall accuracy.

Gaw et al. 2019 ↗
03 / Translation

Ecological opportunity

Gardener & Son translates those two layers into a simple design question: which lost structures and functions could this place support again? This final layer is interpretive, not an official Singapore vegetation classification.

Prototype note. The two published map figures are visually georeferenced here for demonstration, so point readings are indicative rather than parcel-accurate. A production version should query the underlying GIS data directly and should use current site surveys before ecological or planting decisions. Reclaimed land, altered coastlines and changed hydrology need particular care.
