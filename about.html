About Ecological Vegetation Classes

EVC Framework

The Ecological Vegetation Class (EVC) framework is a standardised system used in Victoria to classify native vegetation. Each EVC represents a distinct vegetation community defined by its floristic composition (plant species present), vegetation structure, and environmental attributes.

The framework was developed to provide consistent terminology for vegetation mapping, assessment, and management across the state.

Classification Methodology

EVCs are classified based on surveys of remnant vegetation, analysis of floristic patterns, and correlation with environmental factors including soil type, topography, rainfall, and biogeographic region.

The Victorian Government maintains authoritative EVC mapping datasets, updated as new survey data becomes available.

Spatial Data Limitations

EVC boundaries in spatial datasets represent modelled distributions based on available information. Boundaries are not precise lines but represent transition zones where vegetation communities grade into one another.

Mapped EVCs indicate the pre-1750 vegetation community likely to have occurred at a location. Current vegetation may differ significantly due to historical clearing, modification, or regeneration.

Conservation Status

EVCs are assigned conservation status classifications (e.g., endangered, vulnerable, depleted) based on the extent of remaining vegetation relative to pre-European distribution. These classifications inform planning controls and biodiversity policy.

Data Sources

This tool queries the Victorian Government's Native Vegetation Information System (NV2005_EVCBCS layer) via the Open Data Platform. EVC names, bioregions, and conservation status are sourced directly from this dataset.
